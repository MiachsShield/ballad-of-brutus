# Brutus Visual Guide

Character direction · working canon. This is the project's canonical art reference.
Every image generated for the Ballad of Brutus must be made against this guide.

## Character direction

A young fistfighter, drawn to move. Brutus should read before he is explained:
early-to-mid twenties, stocky and brawny, dressed for a hard island life, and
caught at the instant before impact. The target is **stylized clarity** — not
weathered realism. The register is action-comedy inside the same palette:
Bravely charm and punch timing, not Souls grit, not carnival sparks.

### Definitive Brutus reference — LOCKED

## Design synthesis — one figure, four useful lessons

### Body in motion
The silhouette should carry the action before detail does.

### Economical detail
Let cloth, armor, and tools describe role — not ornament for its own sake.

### Stylized gravity
Readable, humane faces inside a world that still feels dangerous.

## Published references — the useful parts, not a style collage

Each source contributes a different piece of the answer. The goal is not to
imitate any one game; it is to isolate what makes its character art readable,
tactile, and emotionally immediate.

### Tactics Ogre: Reborn — material logic & silhouette (REF 01)
Clothing hangs with weight. Armor is selective, not total. The long verticals
of coats, swords, and boots produce a clear silhouette without turning the
figure into a mass of gear.

- Take: layered cloth over practical armor; muted value groups with one warm accent
- Avoid: copying the reserved, upright pose

### FINAL FANTASY XIV — impact & foreshortening (REF 02)
The near fist owns the frame. Brush marks turn the strike into a wake, and the
face remains readable inside the movement. This is the clearest model for
Brutus in combat.

- Take: hands as the first read; diagonal force through shoulders and hips
- Avoid: overstating magical glow

### Bravely Default II — charm without softness (REF 03)
Faces are open and appealing, proportions are gently compressed, and costumes
carry personality. The lesson is accessibility: Brutus can be dangerous without
looking grim or middle-aged.

- Take: young face, clean emotional read; class identity through costume shape
- Avoid: chibi proportions for the final figure

### Wizardry Variants Daphne — quiet detail & dungeon weight (REF 04)
Fine linework describes construction, closure, hem, and wear while leaving
broad calm spaces. It offers a useful answer to "dark fantasy" that does not
require mud, noise, or photorealism.

- Take: selective costume construction detail; serious tone through restraint
- Avoid: fragile, fashion-first posing

## Direction synthesis — the Brutus blend

Build him from the movement outward. FFXIV supplies the attack grammar;
Tactics Ogre supplies material judgment; Bravely supplies youth and warmth;
Daphne supplies restraint and dungeon-world seriousness.

1. **Pose leads.** Low center of gravity; one hand forward; shoulders already
   turning into the next strike.
2. **Face stays young, reads strong.** Strong jaw, sharp eyes, little
   facial weathering. Brutus is in his twenties, not a veteran carved from
   stone.
3. **Cloth does the storytelling.** Island leather, wrapped hands, a broken-in
   jacket, and a small amount of metal where it earns its weight.
4. **Color is disciplined.** Ink, soot-purple, oxblood, weathered brass, and
   bone cloth. Saturation appears at the point of action.
5. **Render for recognition.** Large shape first, action second, costume third.
   Small detail must never bury the class fantasy.

### Push toward
- Stylized anatomy with credible weight
- Expressive hands and forearms
- Uneven, lived-in garment layers
- A readable action at thumbnail size
- Warm humanity inside a severe world
- Punch timing and charm — action-comedy energy, never grimdark

### Pull away from
- Weathered middle-aged hero face
- Souls grit, grim oil-painting mood, carnival sparks
- Generic sword-and-breastplate adventurer
- Photoreal pores, grime, or cinematic grit
- Symmetrical idle stance
- Decorative straps with no function

## Character specification

- **Age & expression:** early-to-mid twenties. Strong jaw, sharp eyes.
  Focus reads as curiosity under pressure, not permanent scowling. Still
  young — never a weathered veteran.
- **Build:** stocky, brawny, thick chest and arms. Strong hands and shoulders;
  moves like a fighter, not a bodybuilder posing.
- **Combat identity:** pugilist first. Fists, elbows, shoulder checks, braced
  forearms. A weapon should extend the hand — not replace it.
- **Costume logic:** asymmetrical wraps, short outer layer, high movement at
  knee and hip, island-worn leather, restrained metal.
- **Rendering:** stylized line and painterly block-in. Edge hierarchy over
  surface texture. Keep the eyes and striking hand crisp.

### Action test
At card size, a viewer should name the move in under a second: strike, guard,
lunge, break, recover.

## Core palette

Near-black anchors the silhouette; soot-purple gives identity; oxblood carries
heat; weathered brass marks rank; bone cloth keeps the figure legible against
dungeons.

- Ink
- Soot violet
- Oxblood
- Old brass
- Bone cloth

## Production use — how the guide moves into art

The same visual grammar should survive across hero art, dialogue portraits, and
ability cards — even when the level of detail changes.

- **A · Key art:** show the whole kinetic sentence. One dominant strike, a
  counter-curve in coat or sash, and enough environment to imply the sealed
  island without stealing the frame.
- **B · Portraits:** preserve youth and intelligence. Use cleaner planes than
  the key art. Change expression before changing costume detail; recognition
  lives in face shape and eyes.
- **C · Ability cards:** the action is the illustration. Crop aggressively
  around fist, target, and consequence. High-cost actions get broader motion,
  deeper recovery, and a more forceful frame break.

## Card-readability standard (Robert's test)

A card's effect must be visually clear and readable from the art itself.
Stronger abilities must visually read as more expensive: more energy cost,
windup, or recovery frames. Every card face is reviewed against this test.

## Dungeon character rendering — style target (Robert, 2026-09-16)

`dungeon-character-style-reference.jpg` is the visual target for how
characters should look **inside the dungeon** (Brutus, companions, enemies).

- Stylized anime 3D in the Wizardry Variants Daphne register: clean
  readable sculpts, expressive faces, cloth and gear that describe the
  role at a glance. This is a render-style target, not a redesign —
  the locked Brutus spec (early-to-mid 20s, shaved/close buzz, stocky
  and brawny, strong jaw) still governs Brutus himself.
- The figure in the reference is a short female — she is NOT Brutus.
  Take the style, the proportions-in-space, and the readability, not
  the character.
- Characters must read at corridor scale: silhouette and role clear
  from the player's camera distance, against stone-dungeon geometry.
- Same action-comedy register as the rest of the guide: Bravely charm
  and punch timing, not Souls grit.
