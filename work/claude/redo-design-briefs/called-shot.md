# Redo brief — Called Shot

- **Name:** Called Shot
- **Class:** Ranger
- **Energy cost / rules text:** Not canon; card doesn't exist in the
  build at all. Only citable numbers are my own audit-design row (design,
  not canon): light tier, cost 3, "6+cripple" — "a precise called shot to
  the leg."

## Readability failure (original face, `art/audit/faces/called-shot.jpg`)

CRITICAL / ship-blocking: the art is a baseball pitcher mid-windup next to
an anatomical knee-joint diagram with a pointer arrow, captioned "KNEE
JOINT." This is wrong-universe modern-sport imagery in a dark-fantasy
island setting — no baseball uniform, pitcher's mound, or clinical
anatomy chart belongs anywhere in this game. It also shows no ranged
weapon at all, despite this being a Ranger card.

## Art direction

- **Action:** a Ranger drawing and firing a bow at a precise, named weak
  point on an enemy — target-lock on a specific joint/gap in armor, not a
  labeled anatomy chart.
- **Crop:** per "C · Ability cards," crop to the drawn bow/hand and the
  impact point on the target. The "precision" read comes from the arrow
  visibly finding a gap (visor slit, joint, unarmored seam), not a
  diagram callout.
- **Cost read:** light tier, cost 3 — cheap and fast. Draw and impact
  should read quick and economical, not an elaborate multi-panel diagram.
- **Hard rule:** zero modern real-world sport iconography and zero
  clinical/textbook diagram elements anywhere in frame — this is the
  exact failure being corrected.

## Visual-guide citations

- "Avoid: generic sword-and-breastplate adventurer" — extends here to
  "avoid any real-world iconography that breaks the setting."
- Action test: "name the move in under a second: strike, guard, lunge,
  break, recover" — Called Shot reads closest to a precision strike.

precision read (arrow finding the visor gap) is exactly the "called shot
to a weak point" concept the name implies. **Agree with Kavi's PASS
verdict.**

## Pass test

A viewer reads "ranged precision strike at a called weak point" in under
