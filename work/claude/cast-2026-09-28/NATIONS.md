# Seven nations — ideal melds

**Status: Tier B proposal (zero canon), except where it restates Robert's locked
rulings.** Written 2026-10-01 from Robert's phenotype-basis table (Marium Roman ·
Themelios Grecian · Reyjar Norse-Spanish · Li Trice French-Chinese · Ayusti
Japanese-Brazilian · Beloufi Americana-Irish · Edinius English-Lithuanian).
**Where this document conflicts with `canon/world-bible-v3.md` §4.6 (Robert's locked
rulings on the seven peoples), the world bible wins.** This revision follows those
rulings.

## How to read this

1. **Ideal fusion, not realism.** Each mixed nation takes the most distinctive,
   most beautiful features of its two inspirations and melts them into one
   coherent people. Nothing here has to be historically plausible, and none of it
   needs a backstory to justify it.
2. **A meld is a third thing**, never one culture in the other's clothes. Names,
   dress, buildings and manner are all designed as one fused style. In the five
   mixed nations there are no pure-source names at all.
3. **Ranges, not types.** Looks say what is *common*, never what is *required*.
   Any adventurer can break any of it.
4. **Culture identifies, phenotype only colours.** Name, dress and manner tell
   you where someone is from before their face does.
5. **Marium and Themelios** have one inspiration each (Marium is the Roman ideal made
   brawnier by island life; Themelios is Greece-ish, made diverse by merchants).
6. **Name pools.** `NAMES.md` lists 150+ given names per nation. `names.js` holds
   the family and house names.
7. **Physical features.** `LOOKS.md` elaborates every "Looks" line below: face, eyes,
   skin, hair, build, hands and marks, aging and variation, plus an art cheat-sheet.

## Marium — Roman, ideal
- **Looks:** Roman, but brawnier and bronzed by island life. Dark brown to black
  wavy hair; deep sun-and-sea bronze; dark eyes, some hazel; strong level brows, a
  straight or high-bridged nose; broad, thick-necked, heavy-armed builds. Even
  Brutus's peers think him exceptionally vigorous: he seems to overflow with life
  force (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Names:** given name + family name (-ius / -ia) + a **cognomen**, the earned
  byname from a look or a deed (Rufus, "the red"), which is the celebrity layer.
  *Families:* Aurelius, Cornelius, Fabius, Claudius, Domitius, Flavius, Livius,
  Septimius.
- **Dress and places:** wool tunic and cloak with a coloured border for rank, cut
  for work; marble forums, aqueduct bridges, mosaic floors, bathhouses, and a harbour
  at the end of every street.
- **Bearing:** formal, law-minded, proud of civic duty and of physical strength.
- **Fits:** Lantern Company.

## Themelios — Grecian, merchant crossroads
- **Looks:** Greece-ish, but a lot more diverse because merchants run it: the base
  look is the Mediterranean line (a straight profile, dark curls, olive skin), yet
  every complexion, build and style turns up in the agora. Expect the widest range
  of any nation (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Names:** compound names that mean something (Nikandros "victory of men",
  Eudora "good gift"); patronymic in -ides. *Families:* Kleonides, Demetrides,
  Theodorides, Philippides, Aristides, Alexides.
- **Dress and places:** layered, pinned and draped cloth in bright trade colours
  from every direction; whitewashed counting-houses, theatres cut into hillsides,
  olive groves.
- **Bearing:** warm, pleasant and quick with an invitation, and just as quick with
  a damaging rumour. Do not let the manners fool you (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Fits:** The Hollow Crown (a court of fallen aristocrats), Nikandros.

## Reyjar — Norse-Spanish
- **The meld:** the pale north. Porcelain, black lace and iron: a cold, slender,
  watchful people, formal to the point of ceremony.
- **Looks:** porcelain, cold skin. Hair from white-blonde through ash-grey to jet
  black. High cheekbones, slightly sunken (deep-set) eyes, slender, relatively tall
  (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Names:** Norse weight with Iberian music. Norse stems wear Spanish endings
  (Halvardo, Sigruna, Bjornardo); Spanish stems get Norse bite (Ramund, Velhild);
  and the Spanish *e-* before an *s* cluster (Esteinar, Estrida). The family name
  is an **-ez** patronymic for everyone (Halvardez). *Families:* Halvardez,
  Torvaldez, Eirikez, Ottarez, Sigurdez, Ragnez, Gudmundez.
- **Dress:** dark wool cape-cloaks lined in crimson or saffron against the pale
  skin, pinned with a silver brooch; black lace; short fitted jackets; wide leather
  sashes; women in braided crowns under black lace.
- **Places:** long halls of pale stone around cool courtyards with fountains,
  dragon-prow beams, slate roofs.
- **Bearing:** ceremony, restraint and a long memory for slights.
- **Fits:** The Iron Oath, Hask (who has dropped his family name).

## Li Trice — French-Chinese
- **The meld:** the city of lanterns and lattice, and the island's forefront of
  fashion: dramatic colour and the contoured body, worn with composure.
- **Looks:** petite to average height. Women full through the hip and thigh; men
  stockier yet agile. Hair from almond-brown to black; eyes red-brown to black;
  beauty marks on the face, arms and legs (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Names:** soft-vowelled, nasal and liquid. Chinese syllables take French
  endings (Lanette, Wenaud), French stems take Chinese endings (Mirlan, Sorwen),
  and some names sit natively in both mouths (Yann, Mai, Luce). **Two orders, both
  correct:** house first in writing and ceremony (Laque Aline), given name first
  in speech (Aline Laque); which you use says who you are talking to. *Houses:*
  Laque (lacquer), Soie (silk), Encre (ink), Jonque (junk boat), Sel (salt), Papier
  (paper), Lanterne.
- **Dress:** cut close to the body in fluid, contoured lines (wrap-and-sheath
  tailoring, bias-cut panels, a defined waist and hip) in dramatic saturated colour:
  vermilion, emerald, deep indigo, black lacquer, gold thread. A new cut that is
  neither one source nor the other. The island copies it a season late.
- **Places:** carved lattice screens and wrought-iron balconies, canals of
  lanterns, arcaded tea-salons, dressmakers' windows.
- **Bearing:** elaborate courtesy, deals sealed with tea and wine, a reputation
  is a house name, and everyone is dressed to be seen.
- **Fits:** Gilt Hand, Aline Laque, Remy Jonque.

## Ayusti — Japanese-Brazilian
- **The meld:** the lantern coast. Meticulous craft and unembarrassed joy, where
  every festival has drums and paper lanterns in the same parade.
- **Looks:** light tan, energetic, extroverted and cute; petite to average height;
  faces lean Japanese (locked, bible §4.6). Hair straight black to dark brown, bright
  dark eyes, quick smiles.
- **Names:** both sound-worlds are open-vowelled, so names are crisp and
  sing-song. Japanese stems take a Brazilian lilt (Yukinha, Takinho, Sorazinha),
  Portuguese stems take a Japanese one (Mariko, Caetaro), and some names are new
  from both (Takari, Sorano). Family names are **doubled**: a place named in both
  tongues and fused (Kawario, river-river). *Families:* Kawario, Moriselva,
  Yamaserra, Ishipedra, Umimar, Hoshiestrela.
- **Dress:** bright cotton wraps and sashes in bold prints; indigo and tropical
  colour; wide woven hats; cloth tied into knotted carrying bundles; ribbons and
  small charms at festivals.
- **Places:** wooden houses with paper screens and deep shaded verandas hung with
  hammocks and lanterns; terraced hills; busy fish markets.
- **Bearing:** loud, warm, quick to befriend; everything is a festival.
- **Fits:** The Brethren of Saint Piran, Ines and Ivo Kawario.

## Beloufi — Americana-Irish
- **The meld:** the porch and the hearth. Giants who love hard and feast harder, fiercely
  loyal to the people they care about and hard on everyone else, the least united
  people on the island.
- **Looks:** six to seven feet tall and well endowed: the men muscular even at that
  height, the women generously proportioned without extra chunkiness (locked, bible
  §4.6). Hair from copper and auburn to black, any eye colour, ruddy cheeks and laugh
  lines.
- **Names:** clan names worn as first names (Brennan, Keegan), ogham trees and
  prairie plants (Rowan, Juniper, Linnet), and saints and scripture (Patrick,
  Malachy, Hannah). **Bynames are tall-tale nicknames** ("Hardtack", "Two-Bucket"),
  the celebrity layer. *Families:* Calloway, Tolliver, O'Dell, Brannock,
  McTierney, Harkness, Gallagher, Quill (the scribe).
- **Dress:** homespun and canvas cut generously, patched quilted vests worked with
  knotwork, wide-brimmed hats with a sprig in the band, sturdy boots, plaid wool; a
  quilt makes a fine cloak.
- **Places:** stone cottages with doorways cut tall and deep porches, a fiddle or
  banjo in every doorway, barn dances, pubs that are also general stores.
- **Bearing:** love shown loudly to those they care about, merriment as a way of life,
  blunt dispute settling, and quick, severe retribution on anyone who lies to,
  embarrasses, humiliates or betrays them. Loyalty is personal, not national: Beloufi
  have no common love for one another, and vitriol toward liars and betrayers makes
  them the least united of the seven regions. They are not savages (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Fits:** The Merry Rabble, Mabel Quill.

## Edinius — English-Lithuanian
- **The meld:** the amber north. Tall, pale, direct and dry: people who say a thing
  once and mean it.
- **Looks:** pale white skin, tall, Lithuanian face shape, and light green eyes.
  Light green is also a national colour, alongside pale white (locked, bible §4.6).
- **Names:** English heroic stems in Lithuanian dress (Godrikas, Atelstanas),
  Lithuanian stems with English iron (Gedwin, Algric, Laimhild), diminutives both
  ways (Wulfukas, Hildute), and Germanic names in two spellings (Edmund,
  Edmundas). Families are an English stem plus **-aitis / -aite** ("son /
  daughter of"). *Families:* Godricaitis, Wulfaitis, Oswinaitis, Aldraitis,
  Edmundaitis, Harraitis.
- **Dress:** conservative and dignified in muted tones, with pale white and light
  green as the national colours; light green appears somewhere on every outfit (a
  lining, ribbon, trim or pin); wool kirtles and long coats; one amber bead is plenty.
- **Places:** timber halls with steep thatched roofs and carved sun-wheels; sacred
  groves with a hearth-fire at the centre; plain, orderly amber markets.
- **Bearing:** direct, curt, dry-humoured, dignified.
- **Fits:** The Black Candle, Ember, Wren.
