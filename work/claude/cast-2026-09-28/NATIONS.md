# Seven nations — ideal melds

**Status: Tier B proposal (zero canon).** Written 2026-10-01 from Robert's
phenotype-basis table (Marium Roman · Themelios Grecian · Reyjar Norse-Spanish ·
Li Trice French-Chinese · Ayusti Japanese-Brazilian · Beloufi Americana-Irish ·
Edinius English-Lithuanian). The world bible has the final word.

## How to read this

1. **Ideal fusion, not realism.** Each mixed nation takes the most distinctive,
   most beautiful features of its two inspirations and melts them into one
   coherent people. Nothing here has to be historically plausible, and none of it
   needs a backstory to justify it.
2. **A meld is a third thing**, never one culture in the other's clothes. Names,
   dress, buildings and manner are all designed as one fused style. In the five
   mixed nations there are no pure-source names at all.
3. **Ranges, not types.** Looks say what is *common*, never what is *required*.
   Any adventurer can break any of it.
4. **Culture identifies, phenotype only colours.** Name, dress and manner tell
   you where someone is from before their face does.
5. **Marium and Themelios** have one inspiration each, so they are the *ideal* of
   it: Rome and Hellas as they dreamed themselves.
6. **Name pools.** `NAMES.md` lists 150+ given names per nation. `names.js` holds
   the family and house names.

## Marium — Roman, ideal
- **Looks:** dark brown to black hair, straight to wavy; olive complexion from
  light to deep tan; dark eyes, some hazel; strong brows, a straight or
  high-bridged nose; compact, sturdy builds.
- **Names:** given name + family name (-ius / -ia) + a **cognomen**, the earned
  byname from a look or a deed (Rufus, "the red"), which is the celebrity layer.
  *Families:* Aurelius, Cornelius, Fabius, Claudius, Domitius, Flavius, Livius,
  Septimius.
- **Dress and places:** wool tunic and cloak with a coloured border for rank;
  marble forums, aqueduct bridges, mosaic floors, bathhouses.
- **Bearing:** formal, law-minded, proud of civic duty.
- **Fits:** Lantern Company.

## Themelios — Grecian, ideal
- **Looks:** olive complexion, dark wavy to curly hair, dark eyes, a straight or
  gently aquiline nose bridge; lean, athletic builds.
- **Names:** compound names that mean something (Nikandros "victory of men",
  Eudora "good gift"); patronymic in -ides. *Families:* Kleonides, Demetrides,
  Theodorides, Philippides, Aristides, Alexides.
- **Dress and places:** draped, pinned wool with a bold border; whitewashed
  terraces, theatres cut into hillsides, olive groves.
- **Bearing:** talkers, debaters, athletes, generous to strangers.
- **Fits:** The Hollow Crown (a court of fallen aristocrats), Nikandros.

## Reyjar — Norse-Spanish
- **The meld:** the sunlit north. A sea-king people with a pageant's sense of
  occasion: the saga sung to a guitar, the assembly held in a plaza at dusk, honour
  worn like a cloak.
- **Looks:** dark-browed and light-eyed: strong dark brows over grey, green or
  amber eyes. Skin sun-bronzed, flushing red at the cheekbones. Hair dark
  chestnut to black with copper in the sun, braided with ribbons. Tall, rangy,
  straight-backed, with a dancer's carriage.
- **Names:** Norse weight with Iberian music. Norse stems wear Spanish endings
  (Halvardo, Sigruna, Bjornardo); Spanish stems get Norse bite (Ramund, Velhild);
  and the Spanish *e-* before an *s* cluster (Esteinar, Estrida). The family name
  is an **-ez** patronymic for everyone (Halvardez). *Families:* Halvardez,
  Torvaldez, Eirikez, Ottarez, Sigurdez, Ragnez, Gudmundez.
- **Dress:** dark wool cape-cloaks lined in saffron or crimson, pinned with a
  silver brooch; short embroidered jackets; wide leather sashes; riding boots with
  silver spurs; women in braided crowns under black lace.
- **Places:** long halls built around sunlit courtyards with fountains, dragon-prow
  beams, tiled roofs.
- **Bearing:** ceremony, boast, and a good sense of occasion.
- **Fits:** The Iron Oath, Dagny, Hask (who has dropped his family name).

## Li Trice — French-Chinese
- **The meld:** the city of lanterns and lattice, where refinement is an art
  form. Courtesy, wit, ink and perfume, all of it understated and exact.
- **Looks:** porcelain-to-gold complexions; dark glossy hair, straight or in soft
  waves, often silvering early; fine features and lidded, luminous eyes (dark
  brown to amber, sometimes grey); slender, graceful, upright. The ideal beauty is
  composure. Never draw half-and-half: draw individuals.
- **Names:** soft-vowelled, nasal and liquid. Chinese syllables take French
  endings (Lanette, Wenaud), French stems take Chinese endings (Mirlan, Sorwen),
  and some names sit natively in both mouths (Yann, Mai, Luce). **Two orders, both
  correct:** house first in writing and ceremony (Laque Aline), given name first
  in speech (Aline Laque); which you use says who you are talking to. *Houses:*
  Laque (lacquer), Soie (silk), Encre (ink), Jonque (junk boat), Sel (salt), Papier
  (paper), Lanterne.
- **Dress:** a new cut, neither one nor the other: light layered coats, close at
  the shoulder, falling in clean panels; high stand collars closed with an enamel
  button and a silk knot at once; sleeves long enough to hide a fan; ink, celadon
  and vermilion; one lacquered hair pin.
- **Places:** carved lattice screens and wrought-iron balconies, canals of
  lanterns, arcaded tea-salons.
- **Bearing:** elaborate courtesy, deals sealed with tea and wine, a reputation
  is a house name.
- **Fits:** Gilt Hand, Aline Laque, Remy Jonque.

## Ayusti — Japanese-Brazilian
- **The meld:** the lantern coast. Meticulous craft and unembarrassed joy, where
  every festival has drums and paper lanterns in the same parade.
- **Looks:** sun-warmed skin from light to golden-brown; hair from straight black
  to wavy dark brown, worn loose or tied in bright cloth; bright dark eyes and
  quick smiles; slight to sturdy, supple and athletic; neat, quick hands.
- **Names:** both sound-worlds are open-vowelled, so names are crisp and
  sing-song. Japanese stems take a Brazilian lilt (Yukinha, Takinho, Sorazinha),
  Portuguese stems take a Japanese one (Mariko, Caetaro), and some names are new
  from both (Takari, Sorano). Family names are **doubled**: a place named in both
  tongues and fused (Kawario, river-river). *Families:* Kawario, Moriselva,
  Yamaserra, Ishipedra, Umimar, Hoshiestrela.
- **Dress:** bright cotton wraps and sashes in bold prints; indigo and tropical
  colour; wide woven hats; cloth tied into knotted carrying bundles; ribbons at
  festivals.
- **Places:** wooden houses with paper screens and deep shaded verandas hung with
  hammocks and lanterns; terraced hills; busy fish markets.
- **Bearing:** loud warm hospitality over careful craft; everything is a festival.
- **Fits:** The Brethren of Saint Piran, Ines and Ivo Kawario.

## Beloufi — Americana-Irish
- **The meld:** the porch and the hearth. Song, story and a long memory, where
  the Irish tale and the frontier tall tale are the same tale told twice.
- **Looks:** sun-lined and freckled; hair from copper and auburn to black; any eye
  colour; lean, weathered, strong hands; ruddy cheeks and laugh lines.
- **Names:** clan names worn as first names (Brennan, Keegan), ogham trees and
  prairie plants (Rowan, Juniper, Linnet), and saints and scripture (Patrick,
  Malachy, Hannah). **Bynames are tall-tale nicknames** ("Hardtack", "Two-Bucket"),
  the celebrity layer. *Families:* Calloway, Tolliver, O'Dell, Brannock,
  McTierney, Harkness, Gallagher, Quill (the scribe).
- **Dress:** homespun and canvas, patched quilted vests worked with knotwork,
  wide-brimmed hats with a sprig in the band, sturdy boots, plaid wool; a quilt
  makes a fine cloak.
- **Places:** stone cottages with deep porches, a fiddle or banjo in every
  doorway, barn dances, pubs that are also general stores.
- **Bearing:** blunt hospitality, dry humour, long feuds, wakes that are parties.
- **Fits:** The Merry Rabble, Mabel Quill.

## Edinius — English-Lithuanian
- **The meld:** the amber north. Wool and forest and a banked fire: slow friendships
  and deep loyalty.
- **Looks:** fair and rosy, windburned; hair ash-blond through amber to copper-brown;
  eyes grey, blue, green or hazel; broad cheekbones and strong jaws; sturdy.
- **Names:** English heroic stems in Lithuanian dress (Godrikas, Atelstanas),
  Lithuanian stems with English iron (Gedwin, Algric, Laimhild), diminutives both
  ways (Wulfukas, Hildute), and Germanic names in two spellings (Edmund,
  Edmundas). Families are an English stem plus **-aitis / -aite** ("son /
  daughter of"). *Families:* Godricaitis, Wulfaitis, Oswinaitis, Aldraitis,
  Edmundaitis, Harraitis.
- **Dress:** wool kirtles and long coats; woven patterned sashes edged in
  heraldic borders; strings of amber beads.
- **Places:** timber halls with steep thatched roofs and carved sun-wheels; sacred
  groves with a hearth-fire at the centre; amber markets.
- **Bearing:** reserved, dry-humoured, stubborn, reverent toward grove and hearth.
- **Fits:** The Black Candle, Ember, Wren.
