# Seven nations — features, looks and names

**Status: Tier B proposal (zero canon).** Written 2026-10-01 from Robert's
phenotype-basis table (Marium Roman · Themelios Grecian · Reyjar Norse-Spanish ·
Li Trice French-Chinese · Ayusti Japanese-Brazilian · Beloufi Americana-Irish ·
Edinius English-Lithuanian). The in-world histories below are invented to make
the melds hold together; the world bible has the final word.

## How to read this

1. **Ranges, not types.** Looks say what is *common* in a nation, never what is
   *required*. Any adventurer can break any of it.
2. **A meld is a third thing.** For the five mixed nations, names, dress and
   custom come from an invented history in which the two sources actually met.
   They are not one people in the other's clothes. Wherever the two traditions
   already rhyme (a shared name, a shared meaning, a shared institution), the
   people grew from that.
3. **Culture identifies, phenotype only colours.** Name, dress, custom and
   manner tell you where someone is from before their face does.
4. **Single-source nations** (Marium, Themelios) get their distinctiveness from
   internal variety instead of a second source.

| Nation | Basis | Where the two meet |
|---|---|---|
| Marium | Roman | single source: old city families, farm-country Italics, harbour provincials |
| Themelios | Grecian | single source: city-states, island sailors, mountain shepherds |
| Reyjar | Norse-Spanish | a seafaring warrior gentry that settled sun-baked uplands |
| Li Trice | French-Chinese | a port republic run by trading houses |
| Ayusti | Japanese-Brazilian | a monsoon coast of settlers and a mixed local people |
| Beloufi | Americana-Irish | a frontier of Irish settlers and every other newcomer |
| Edinius | English-Lithuanian | a cold forest north of wool, amber and shared rivers |

## Marium — Roman
- **Looks:** dark brown to black hair, straight to wavy; olive complexion from
  light to deep tan; dark eyes, some hazel; strong brows, a straight or
  high-bridged nose; compact, sturdy builds.
- **Names:** given name + family name (-ius / -ia) + a **cognomen**. The cognomen
  is the celebrity layer: an earned byname from a look or a deed (Rufus, "the
  red"). Women take the family name (Cornelia).
  *Given:* Lucan, Aurelia, Cassian, Livia, Marcellus, Valeria. *Families:*
  Aurelius, Cornelius, Fabius, Claudius, Domitius, Flavius, Livius, Septimius.
- **Dress and bearing:** wool tunic and cloak, rank shown by a coloured border;
  formal, law-minded, proud of civic duty and of a good bathhouse.
- **Fits:** Lantern Company.

## Themelios — Grecian
- **Looks:** olive complexion, dark wavy to curly hair, dark eyes, a straight or
  gently aquiline nose bridge; lean, athletic builds are common.
- **Names:** compound names that mean something: Nikandros "victory of men",
  Eudora "good gift", Theron "hunter". A patronymic in -ides (Kleonides, "of
  Kleon's line"). Bynames name a city or a deed ("of Themelios", "the Wise").
  *Families:* Kleonides, Demetrides, Theodorides, Philippides, Aristides, Alexides.
- **Dress and bearing:** draped, pinned wool with a bold border; talkers and
  debaters, athletes, great hosts to strangers.
- **Fits:** The Hollow Crown (a court of fallen aristocrats), Nikandros.

## Reyjar — Norse-Spanish
- **How they meet:** a seafaring warrior gentry settled sun-baked uplands and kept
  its law-assembly beside Iberian town charters. The pattern is real: Spain's own
  names (Rodrigo, Alvaro, Gonzalo, Elvira, Alonso) are Germanic in origin. Reyjar
  simply kept the roots closer to the north.
- **Looks:** tall and sturdy are common; hair from dark brown through chestnut
  to auburn or fair; eyes grey, hazel, green or brown; complexion fair to olive,
  weathered, quick to tan or burn.
- **Names:** Norse and Iberian-Germanic given names side by side. The family name
  is a patronymic in **-ez** for every gender (Halvardez, "of Halvard's line").
  *Given:* Halvard, Sigrun, Eirik, Ragna, Alvaro, Elvira, Rodrigo, Gonzalo.
  *Families:* Halvardez, Torvaldez, Eirikez, Ottarez, Sigurdez, Ragnez, Gudmundez.
- **Dress and bearing:** wool cloaks fastened with a brooch, embroidered sashes,
  leather and steel; formal honour, recited deeds, pride of lineage.
- **Fits:** The Iron Oath, Dagny, Hask (who has dropped his family name).

## Li Trice — French-Chinese
- **How they meet:** a port republic where trading houses, not lineages, are the
  unit of society, the way creole port cities actually grow. Houses are named for
  the goods their founders carried, and several of those words are French because
  French learned the goods through this trade.
- **Looks:** a continuum shaped by centuries of marriage. Hair black to dark brown,
  straight to wavy, sometimes chestnut; skin from pale ivory through warm gold to
  light brown; eyes mostly dark brown, some hazel or grey; eyelid shapes from
  monolid to double-lidded; faces broad to narrow. **Do not draw half-and-half.**
  Draw individuals, some leaning one way, most visibly neither.
- **Names:** *convergence* given names, equally at home in both mouths: Yann (Yan),
  Mai, Luce (Lu), Lanne (Lan + Anne), Meline (Mei + Mélanie), Liane (Lian), Linet
  (Lin), Aline (Lin). House names in French trade words. **Two orders, both
  correct:** house first in writing and ceremony (Laque Aline), given name first
  in speech (Aline Laque). Which you use says who you are talking to.
  *Houses:* Laque (lacquer), Soie (silk), Encre (ink), Jonque (junk boat), Sel
  (salt), Papier (paper), Lanterne.
- **Dress and bearing:** cut by climate and trade, not heritage: light layered
  silk and linen, long sleeves with a house-coloured cuff, a ledger pouch at the
  belt, fastenings that are knots and buttons interchangeably. Deals are sealed
  with tea and with wine. Courtesy that saves everyone's face, salon wit on top.
  A reputation is a house name.
- **Fits:** Gilt Hand, Aline Laque, Remy Jonque.
- **Reading:** the region's own name is already a meld. *Li* is an Occitan article
  and a Chinese word (a ward, a measure of road); *Trice* leans Latin.

## Ayusti — Japanese-Brazilian
- **How they meet:** a monsoon coast where Japanese settler families and an
  already mixed, Portuguese-speaking local people (Indigenous, African and European
  roots) built one new people, not two neighbouring communities. "Brazilian" here
  is a continuum, not a type.
- **Looks:** hair from straight black through wavy brown to tight curls; skin from
  light to deep brown; eyes mostly dark; eyelid shapes vary; builds from slight to
  stocky. There is no Ayusti type.
- **Names:** *convergence* given names that sit in both languages: Rui, Iuri
  (Yuri), Nina, Caio, Aya, Mayara, Mika; Ine and Inês (Ine is the rice plant), Ivo
  and Iyo. Ordinary names from either side sit beside them (Haru, Takeo, Beatriz,
  Luzia). **Doubled house names:** settlers named places in both languages and
  fused them, the way real maps end up with "Torpenhow Hill" (hill-hill-hill).
  Ayusti families do the same: Kawario (*kawa* + *rio*, river-river), Moriselva
  (forest-forest), Yamaserra (mountain-mountain), Ishipedra (stone-stone), Umimar
  (sea-sea), Hoshiestrela (star-star).
- **Dress and bearing:** light wraps and sashes in bold prints, wide hats for sun
  and rain, festival colour with lanterns and ribbons both; loud warm hospitality
  over meticulous craft; the lantern dance and the saint's-day party are one
  festival.
- **Fits:** The Brethren of Saint Piran, Ines and Ivo Kawario.

## Beloufi — Americana-Irish
- **How they meet:** a frontier where Irish settlers and every other kind of
  newcomer were shaken together over generations, the way folk America was: clan
  and story on one side, fiddle, revival tent, quilt and tall tale on the other.
- **Looks:** a continuum: freckled-fair to deep-brown complexions, hair from red
  and auburn through black to tight curls, any eye colour; lean, weathered builds
  and sun lines.
- **Names:** Biblical and virtue names (Silas, Amos, Eben, Hepzibah, Prudence)
  beside Irish ones (Cormac, Niall, Aoife, Maeve). Irish clan prefixes (Mc, O')
  fastened to frontier trades and places. **Bynames are tall-tale nicknames**
  ("Hardtack", "Two-Bucket"), which is the celebrity layer.
  *Families:* Calloway, Tolliver, O'Dell, Brannock, McTierney, Harkness,
  Gallagher, Quill (the scribe).
- **Dress and bearing:** homespun and canvas, quilted vests, brimmed hats, every
  patch a story; singers and storytellers, blunt hospitality, long feuds.
- **Fits:** The Merry Rabble, Mabel Quill.

## Edinius — English-Lithuanian
- **How they meet:** a cold forested north where yeoman English settlers and
  amber-trading Baltic forest peoples shared the same rivers. Germanic names already
  live in both languages (Edmund / Edmundas, Henry / Henrikas, Alfred / Alfredas),
  so one name has two spellings and people use the one that fits the room.
- **Looks:** a fair palette: pale to ruddy complexion; hair ash-blond, chestnut or
  dark brown with red tints; eyes grey, blue, green or hazel; broad cheekbones
  common; sturdy builds.
- **Names:** English (Oswin, Godric, Hilda), Lithuanian (Vytas, Rasa, Milda, Aldona)
  and the shared Germanic names in either shape (Edmundas, Henrikas). Families are
  an English stem plus Lithuanian **-aitis / -aite** ("son / daughter of"):
  Godricaitis, Wulfaitis, Oswinaitis, Aldraitis, Edmundaitis, Harraitis.
- **Dress and bearing:** wool kirtles and long coats, woven sashes with patterned
  borders, amber beads; reserved, dry-humoured, stubborn, with a reverence for
  groves and hearths.
- **Fits:** The Black Candle, Ember, Wren.
